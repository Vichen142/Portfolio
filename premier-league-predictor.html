Overview

This project combines three ideas: Elo ratings to measure team strength, a Random Forest to predict each match, and a 1,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation that plays the rest of the season over and over to turn those predictions into title odds, qualification chances and relegation risk.

The problem

Predicting one match is not the same as predicting a season. Even a good match model says nothing about the table, because the title race depends on dozens of uncertain results that add up.

I wanted a way to turn match-level probabilities into season-level odds, and to present them in an app that anyone could open and explore without reading code.

How I built it

  1. Rate every team with Elo

    Elo ratings give each team a strength score that moves with results, so recent form counts for more than old history.

  2. Train a match predictor

    A Scikit-Learn Random Forest classifier learns from past matches and returns a probability for each outcome, not just a single guess.

  3. Simulate the season

    For every fixture the simulator draws a result from those probabilities and plays out the whole season. One run is one possible future.

  4. Repeat it 1,000 times

    Running the season 1,000 times produces a spread of possible tables. Counting how often each team wins the title, reaches a qualification place or finishes in the bottom three gives the odds.

  5. Ship it as an app

    A Reflex front end lets anyone open the odds radar and explore the numbers. It is deployed on Reflex Cloud.

What I learned

The key lesson was the gap between predicting a match and predicting a season. The match model is only half of the answer. The simulation is what lets uncertainty add up across the whole fixture list, and running it many times is far more honest than showing one predicted table.

What I would do next: backtest the simulator against past seasons to check that the odds it produces are well calibrated.
